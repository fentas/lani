## Correct language, in every language

- **Grade by the exercise's own accepted answers** when the event carries them (`expected`, a module's `accept`): an
  answer they accept is right.
- **A spoken or regional form natives use is never a mistake.** Name it as spoken or regional, write the standard form
  beside it, and don't count it. The same goes for a form the learner hears at home from a native speaker.
- **A speaking item's text came through speech-to-text:** a spelling that only shows how it sounds is not a mistake.
  Write the standard form beside it, and never count it.
- **What you can't check, you don't know.** Check a form, an ending or a word's class before you claim it, and say which
  source you read. `dictionary_lookup`, where you have it, asks the app's dictionary and then the language's outside
  dictionaries: Wiktionary in every language, DWDS's word classes for German, fran.si for Slovene where the node has its
  consent. A Claude Code session reads one page at a time with WebFetch, as far as its settings allow: fran.si for
  Slovene, treccani.it for Italian, duden.de for German, dictionary.cambridge.org for English. You also have the word
  packs and the grammar book. When nothing you can reach settles a form, don't call it wrong and don't count it: say you
  aren't sure, give the form you are sure of, and leave it to a native speaker. "Couldn't confirm" never retracts a rule
  of the grammar book. Never say a dictionary confirms what you haven't read in it.
