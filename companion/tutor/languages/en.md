## Correct English

- **British English is the standard here, and other standard Englishes are right:** *colour* and *color*, *flat* and
  *apartment*, *have got* and *have*, *gotten* are all fine; say which is whose. Both *realise* and *realize* are British.
- **Register:** English has one *you*. Politeness is in the words (*please*, *could you*, *would you mind*) and in
  names (a first name, or *Mr* or *Mrs* and the surname). A blunt line is a word about manners, never a grammar mistake.
- **Spoken, never counted** (name it, the written form beside it): contractions (*I'm*, *don't*, *gonna*); *less* for
  *fewer*; *who* for *whom*; *me and Tom went*; singular *they*; a sentence that ends in a preposition, or a split
  infinitive (neither is a mistake in writing either). Cumbrian and other dialect (*aye*, *lad*, *lass*, *nowt*, *owt*)
  is named as dialect, and so is what grown-ups say in the North and in Cumbria: *we was*, *it were*, *I seen it*,
  *I'm sat here* (the grading table's `nonstandard`); in a talk the villagers say them too.
- **A mistake is** a missing third-person *-s* (*she go*), a made-up past (*goed*), a wrong tense for the time meant
  (*I am here since Monday*), a missing or wrong article, a wrong preposition, a word order the base language brought
  (*I like very much tea*). A misspelling that keeps the word clear is 🟢 minor.

## A child learning English

A child's first names for everyone are normal: show *Mr* or *Mrs* and *Miss* for the teacher, never count a first name
against them. Praise what was understood before the *-s* or the article, and name no grammar term the school hasn't brought.

## Writing English

- **British English** in the village's lines and spellings; American forms are accepted, in `accept` or as an `ok`
  choice. Cumbrian only as a marked touch, glossed (*lad*, *aye*).
- **A noun without "the"** in packs, its plural in `plural`. Check tenses, articles and prepositions twice.
- **The same line to everyone:** English rarely needs a pair for the learner; use one where the word itself differs
  (`{m:a good lad|f:a good lass}`).
