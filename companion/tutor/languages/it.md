## Correct Italian

- **Register:** *tu* to family, friends and children; *Lei*, its verb in the third person, to grown-ups the learner
  doesn't know and to a teacher. A slip between them is a word about manners, never a grammar mistake. *voi* to one
  person is old-fashioned or southern: understood, not wrong.
- **Standard, whatever older school grammars say:** *lui*, *lei*, *loro* as subjects (never ask for *egli*, *ella*,
  *essi*); *gli* for *a loro*; the passato prossimo for a distant past (the north hardly says the passato remoto: never
  ask for it in speech); *mia mamma* and *la mia mamma* alike.
- **Spoken, never counted** (name it, the written form beside it): *gli* for *le*; *a me mi piace*; *c'ho* for *ho*;
  the indicative after *penso che* (*penso che è vero*); the imperfect in *se me lo dicevi, venivo*. An exercise on the
  congiuntivo or the periodo ipotetico asks for the written form: there its accepted answers decide.
- **Friulian isn't a mistake and isn't Italian:** *mandi* (a greeting, mostly on parting), *fogolâr*, *frico* are
  Friulian words of the region. Label them so, and never mark them wrong.
- **A mistake is** a wrong article's form (*il zaino* for *lo zaino*), a wrong auxiliary (*ho andato* for *sono
  andato*), a participle or an adjective that doesn't agree, the conditional after *se* (*se avrei* for *se avessi*), a
  wrong preposition. A missing accent or apostrophe is 🟢 minor, also where it makes another word (*e*, *è*).

## A child learning Italian

*tu* to everyone is a child's normal Italian: the villagers say *tu* to them, and they may say *tu* or *Lei* back. Show
*Lei* for the teacher, never count *tu* against them. Explain in their base language as a friend does (German: *du*),
gloss every form you use, and name no grammar term the school hasn't brought.

## Writing Italian

- **A noun with its article** in packs and hints (*la mamma*, *l'uovo*, *lo zaino*), the plural with it (*le uova*).
- **Standard Italian in answers;** the spoken forms above go in `accept` or `almost` and are named in `explain`, never
  as a graded wrong choice. Friulian words are labelled as Friulian. Check articles, agreement and the auxiliary twice.
- **Pairs for the learner:** participles and adjectives agree with them (`{m:stanco|f:stanca}`).
