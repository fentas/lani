## Correct German

- **Austrian standard German is the standard here, and Germany's is standard too:** *Jänner* and *Januar*, *heuer* and
  *dieses Jahr*, *Erdäpfel* and *Kartoffeln*, *Semmel* and *Brötchen*, *Grüß Gott* and *Guten Tag* are all right; say
  which is whose. *ich bin gesessen*, *gestanden*, *gelegen* is right in Austria, *ich habe gesessen* in Germany.
- **Register:** *du* to family, friends and children; *Sie* to grown-ups the learner doesn't know. A slip between them
  is a word about manners, never a grammar mistake.
- **Spoken, never counted** (name it, the written form beside it): the Perfekt where writing has the Präteritum (Austria
  hardly says the Präteritum outside *war*, *hatte* and the modal verbs: never ask for it in speech); *wegen dem Regen*
  for *wegen des Regens*; *ich hab*, *ich geh* without the *-e*; *weil* with the verb second. Carinthian and other
  dialect (*Griaß di*, *Pfiat di*, *ned* for *nicht*) is named as dialect.
- **A mistake is** a wrong gender, case or adjective ending, a verb out of its place (second in a main clause, last
  after *dass*, *weil* in writing), a wrong auxiliary (*ich habe gegangen*), *das* for *dass*. A small first letter on a
  noun, *ss* for *ß*, *ae* for *ä* are 🟢 minor.

## A child learning German

*du* to everyone is a child's normal German: show *Sie* for a stranger, never count *du* against them. Say a case by its
question (*wem? wen?*) before its name, and the article with every noun, as a word that belongs to it.

## Writing German

- **Austrian standard** in the village's lines (Grüß Gott, Semmel, Jänner, heuer); Germany's form is accepted, in
  `accept` or as an `ok` choice. Dialect only as a marked touch, glossed.
- **A noun with its article** in packs and hints (*die Leiter*), the plural with it. Check gender, case, adjective
  endings and the verb's place twice.
- **Pairs for the learner:** an adjective that agrees with them (`{m:ein guter Freund|f:eine gute Freundin}`).
