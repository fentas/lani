## For the native check

Austrian German first. **The first eight are questions no source settled for Austria.**

1. **den Mensch, dem Bub, dem Student, den Bär, dem Soldat, each without its -en** (`herr-kollege`, the table's `nonstandard`): almost, the standard form shown, no mistake recorded, named in step 3 and on the page. The source is general, not Austrian: [Thieroff, Linguistik online 16](https://bop.unibe.ch/linguistik-online/article/download/798/1376/3542) (opened) quotes Wahrig 2003: "Diese Formen gelten als umgangssprachlich", the forms come "ausschließlich bei Substantiven … die im Nominativ Singular auf Konsonant enden", and in the abstract "in the spoken register and sometimes even in the written register". The same article quotes Duden 2001 calling them "nicht korrekt". Does a Carinthian say them? If not, the rules go and these are mistakes.
2. **mit Herr Moser, Ich kenne Herr Moser** (the table, step 4, the page). **No source found**; from memory, and by the class above. A search found only that Herrn is the standard form.
3. **dem Kollege stays wrong**, as Wahrig says the -e nouns don't lose it. The same for dem Kunde, dem Hirte, den Hase, and they are wrong options in choices. Dialect may differ: is any of these said here?
4. **Herren for Herrn and Herrn for Herren** (the table, almost). [Duden, Herr](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Herr), opened: "des Herrn, seltener: Herren", "die Herren, seltener: Herrn". No choice offers them.
5. **dem Bauer, des Bauers; dem Nachbar, des Nachbars; des Bubs; des Bärs** (the table). [Duden, Nachbar](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Nachbar), opened: "des Nachbars" rare. [Duden, Bub](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Bub), opened: Bubs colloquial in the south, Austria and Switzerland. Bauer and Bär by Duden 2001 as Thieroff quotes it (Bauer: "selten"; Bär: "nicht korrekt"). The Duden page for Bauer wasn't reached.
6. **der Hirt beside der Hirte.** The table lets dem Hirt pass as almost; [Duden, Hirte](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Hirte), opened, has Hirt as a related form with no region. The unit teaches Hirte, as the cast has it.
7. **Partizip II of a sein verb before a noun**, taught as "a verb of change, not gehen, laufen, sitzen" (`partizip-adjektiv` step 5, the page). No source looked up. In Austria sitzen, stehen and liegen take sein, so this says *die gesessene Frau* is out; is that right? And **die angekommenen Gäste** bare reads thin to the second reader, so the sentences say **die gerade angekommenen Gäste**, the table and the sort the short form.
8. **Kundmachung for a Kirchtag's notice** (`partizipialattribut` sheet 2). [Duden, Kundmachung](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Kundmachung), opened: österreichisch, Bekanntmachung. But a notice of the Gemeinde's about a Kirchtag, with who pressed the cider: would a village call that a Kundmachung, or an Ankündigung?

Settled by a dictionary, opened: **froh um** is an `accept` beside froh über, [Duden, froh](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/froh): "über/(süddeutsch, österreichisch, schweizerisch:) um etwas froh sein". **der Beamte, ein Beamter**, [Duden, Beamter](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Beamter). **des Buben**, the same Bub page. **des Burschen**, [Duden, Bursche](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Bursche), though that page names no region and der Bursch was not opened.

Not from a dictionary, each an `accept` or `spoken` from memory or from an existing page:
- **Interesse für** beside Interesse an: an `accept`, and for: never a wrong option.
- **wegen dem Regen, trotz dem Regen**, `spoken` on the lines that turn a clause into a phrase, as `praepositionen-genitiv` has them. **wegen Regen, wegen Regens**, `accept`s.
- **vom Hirten, von dem Hirten, dem Hirten sein Hund**, `spoken` where the genitive is asked, as the `genitiv` page has them.
- **was Neues, nix Gutes**, `spoken` on the lines that have them; elsewhere the table's almost. **drauf**, `spoken`.
- **Aufgrund, Infolge des Sturms**, `accept`s.

**Not looked up, and not in the course:** the am-progressive (Ich bin am Kochen). A search found it coexists with beim in Austria ([Stiglbauer and Wittibschlager](https://wlg.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/p_wlg/892021/FSLenz_WittibschlagerStiglbauer.pdf), search extract). It isn't taught here: `beim` is taught for while, and no line asks a progressive. Weber and Hansen's corpus study of the weak nouns ([IDS](https://ids-pub.bsz-bw.de/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/12717/file/Weber_Hansen_Schwankungen_zwischen_schwacher_und_starker_Substantivreflexion_2024.pdf)) stands behind a bot wall and wasn't read. The Variantengrammatik wasn't opened. German B2's level itself is a judgement ([outlines-2](outlines-2.md), "What the syllabi say"); *Profile deutsch* wasn't read here either.

Then the sentences, for naturalness:

- **herr-kollege**: "Der Hund jagt den Hasen"; "Stanko gibt dem Kunden die Rechnung"; "Kathi ruft Herrn Doktor Egger an"; "Tante Mitzi schreibt ihrem Neffen einen Brief"; der Slowene in a list of the course's words; der Glaube as a word of this group.

What each teaches:

- **herr-kollege**: -n in every case but the nominative on masculine nouns in -e for people and animals (der Kollege, der Kunde, der Neffe, der Hirte, der Slowene, der Hase); -en on a few short ones and on people in -ent, -ist, -at, -graf (der Mensch, der Bär, der Bub, der Student, der Tourist, der Polizist); der Herr, der Bauer, der Nachbar with -n; most others none (der Arzt, der Wirt, der Schmied; der Käse a thing); der Name, der Gedanke, der Buchstabe, der Glaube, and das Herz, with -ns in the genitive. The gap text is Kathi's new neighbour, a photographer; the test a tourist who needs a doctor.
- **der-bekannte**: der Bekannte, ein Bekannter, the other cases, die Bekannte; die Verwandten, viele Verwandte; der Angestellte, der Kranke, der Erwachsene, der Fremde, der Deutsche, der Beamte and die Beamtin; der Österreicher, der Kärntner as nouns of their own; etwas Neues, nichts Gutes, viel Schönes; das Beste, alles Gute. - **partizip-adjektiv**: Partizip I with its endings, what is going on; Partizip II, what was done; kochendes and gekochtes against each other; Partizip II of a verb of change with sein, what has happened; those that are plain adjectives by now (die kommende Woche, spannend, geschlossen), with no ending after sein. - **partizipialattribut**: to read above all: the article waits for its noun, the participle stands right before it, what's between belongs to it; into a relative clause by the participle's kind (mostly the present; wurde or hat; ist), and back. The first sheet's table only asks where the noun and the participle are. The gap text is a Kundmachung; the test a page of the paper. Its choices are reading questions (Wer hat gesucht?).

The gap text is Stefan's cider; the test an evening at Kathi's.

## The spoken forms

Never a choice's wrong option or a bank's distractor; `GermanB1cContentTest` reads the whole course to say so, and the choices of `herr-kollege` that test the -n offer forms nobody says (Kollegem, Neffem) or are typed clozes.

- **Almost, the standard form shown, no mistake recorded** (`grading/de.json`, `nonstandard`), as each is the very thing the unit drills: a weak noun with no -e without its ending; Herr, Herren and Herrn for each other; des Bauers, des Nachbars, des Bubs, des Bärs. **The rule needs `after`:** Menschen is also the plural, and nobody says *viele Mensch*, so the form counts only right after den, dem, einen, einem, the possessives, keinen, diesen, jeden, beim, zum, vom, am, im, with at most one word between. *des Student* and *die Student* stay wrong. Each noun is a rule of its own, or dem Mensch would pass for dem Studenten.
- **Right and labelled** (`spoken`), beside the unit's point: was Neues, nix Gutes; drauf; wegen dem Regen, trotz dem Regen, wegen dem Schnee; vom Hirten and dem Hirten sein Hund.
- **A form both have is two `accept`s**: froh über and froh um, Interesse an and für, des Arztes and des Arzts, kommenden and gekommenen.
