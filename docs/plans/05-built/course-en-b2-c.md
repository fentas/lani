## How the units are written

As COURSE.md "Writing a unit" and "The English course", plus what these six found (for COURSE.md's section, at the integration):
- **A rule line is the first sentence and what fits of the ones with forms, and a sentence ends only at a stop outside the bold.** "**I would like a refund.** Prošnja …" is one sentence with what follows it. The 21 steps I rewrote for this had opened on an example and printed little more than the example, and the list of gonna, gotta, wanna fell off the sheet with no check failing: the smoke only asks for one bold form on the line. They open with the rule now, with the example after it. **Read the rule lines of a unit whose point is a list.**
- **A bold form of the base's spelling trips the voice check:** Italian's **il-** read as the article in her English voice, so that one form is plain in the Italian text.
- **A quick question in the base with an English sentence in it is read wholly in her voice** ("Kaj pomeni: Never have I seen such fog."), so a meaning question is English only: "Never have I seen such fog. = ___".
- **At B2, what a sentence can also say is the longest part of a line:** the first read found 40 things, and most were right answers not listed. A polite request has sixteen right forms, so the unit builds them with a small function, not by hand.
- **Choices offering non-words give the answer away** (kindment, informful): a word-building choice offers real words of the family (kindly, kind).
- **Two independent reads paid again:** a fresh agent on the English, another on the three bases, each reading only the dump of the texts. Acted on, unless named under "Not taken".

- Mr Tyson on a farm stays: he is the cast's miller and farmer.
