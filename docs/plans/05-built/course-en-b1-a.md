### The grading (grading/en.json)

**`same`'s order matters after a merge:** the row for Is it not? stands first, before can not → cannot, so Can you not? reaches it. The row for 'd stands after the 'd rows for would.
- `same`: Is it not?, Do you not?, Can she not? with isn't it, don't you, can't she; 'd before a participle is had, also before a past said for one (I'd went), so A2's rows after had reach it; come, run, put and the like are left out, since they read as would too, and such a line lists both; anymore is any more.
- `loose`: Shed gone, id been, almost.
- `nonstandard`: the eight rows named above, each as narrow as the format lets it be. The be rows have a lookahead in `word`: -ing after it, or for or since further on. `after` sees only what stands before a gap, so **a gap can't hold for or since after it**, and typed "am" in "I ___ here since Monday" would stay wrong: so no gap asks that, and the frame gives the auxiliary (I've ___ (know) …), with the contrast on typed lines.
- `wrong`: fed for feed. The rest, a form without its ending (live for lived, know for known, use for used), was wrong already; `EnglishB1aContentTest` holds them.

English A2's `course-en-a2-c` took telled, catched, I've knew and the like out of `nonstandard` after this branch began. Nothing here relies on those, offers them or pins them.
