## For the native check

Austrian German first. **The first five are questions no source settled.**

1. **`der Mann, wo dort steht`** isn't in the course. A teaching sheet says some Upper German dialects use wo as a general relative
   ([deutschunddeutlich SR9c](https://deutschunddeutlich.de/contentLD/GD/GSR9cKommaRelativ.pdf), opened), and an article cites Bavarian `auf die wo …` ([Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/6645/664572238003/664572238003.pdf), opened). Neither names Carinthia. Does a Carinthian say it? If it
   is speech a learner meets, it should become `spoken` on the der, den and dem gaps of `relativsatz`. Today it is graded wrong.
2. **`der Tag, wo`** (`relativsatz-praeposition`), labelled "in speech" and accepted as `spoken` on one line, with `an dem` as the standard. An article says
   the standard allows wo for places only (see Sources), a teaching sheet in a search extract allows time as well; neither is a dictionary. Duden wasn't reached on this.
3. **`trotzdem es regnet`** and **`obwohl ich hab keine Zeit`** (`obwohl-trotzdem`), `spoken` on every gap and line they fit, named in step 6 and on the page, and
   never a wrong option. The sources are German, not Austrian (see Sources), and they describe `obwohl` with the verb second as mostly corrective, after a pause ("obwohl, ich hab schon zwei gegessen"). So a sentence like `Stefan arbeitet, obwohl er ist krank` may not be what people say. It is accepted so that no spoken form is ever marked wrong, but it also lets through the error the unit drills. If a native says it isn't said, take `spoken` off the table cells, the transform lines and the test.
4. **`etwas, das`** beside `etwas, was`: step 6 of `relativsatz-praeposition` says "you'll also read das". After `alles` and `nichts` only `was` is typed as right, and `das`
   typed there is wrong. A school sheet says "meist WAS"; a Swedish student paper's corpus prefers `das` after etwas (search extract). `das` is nowhere offered as a wrong option.
5. **V2 relatives with an indefinite noun** (*Ich kenne einen Mann, der hat ein Haus*): spoken German has them, from memory. No line of these units has an indefinite noun
   before a relative clause, so none needs a `spoken` form, and the choices that offer `der steht dort` have a definite noun. Is that wrong enough to show on the print?

Then the sentences, for naturalness:

- **relativsatz**: "Das Radio, das sie immer mitbringt"; "Der Wein, den die beiden trinken"; "Das Kind, dem der Hund gehört"; "Das Kind, dem der Kuchen schmeckt, isst viel"; a clause
  moved behind the main verb is accepted only on the test's line that doesn't ask for the middle (`Der Zug ist voll, der um acht abfährt`); `Müller Franz` as the cast has it, where Austrian speech may say `der Müller Franz`; `welcher, welche, welches` accepted wherever a pronoun is typed, and called "right, but heavy".
- **relativsatz-praeposition**: "Das Fest, auf das sich alle freuen"; "Der Hut, ohne den Opa Sepp nicht aus dem Haus geht"; "In Klagenfurt, wo Stefan oft ist"; "der Markt, wohin Stefan den Most bringt".
- **adjektiv-alle-faelle**: "wegen schlechten Wetters", "trotz guter Laune" (and that an adjective-only genitive is rare); "mit gutem altem Wein"; "Das Fleisch schmeckt mit gutem Wein besser"; Stefan's letter ("Vielen Dank für den guten Wein!").
- **schnellere**: "Bei Kathi gibt es das bessere Bier"; "Dieser Zug ist am schnellsten" and "der schnellste" as equally right; "Stefan hat einen größeren Keller als Franz"; "Je mehr Most Stefan trinkt, desto lustiger wird er".

What each teaches:

- **relativsatz**: the comma, the pronoun, the verb last; the gender from the noun, the case from its own clause; der, die, das, den, dem, der, die, **denen**, of these forms the only one that isn't the article's; a clause in the middle of the sentence, two verbs and a separable verb in it. The gap text is Kathi telling a guest who is who; the test is Florian on the Alm. No dessen or deren: not in the outline's line, and B2's business.
- **relativsatz-praeposition**: the preposition first, deciding the case; mit dem, für den; auf dem against auf den by the verb in the clause; a verb's own preposition (warten auf, Angst haben vor: A2's `warten-auf`); no contraction; wo for a place, wohin; was after alles, nichts, etwas and a superlative used as a noun. The gap text is Kathi showing her inn; the test is Stefan's cellar.
- **adjektiv-alle-faelle**: one table, twelve rows, and one rule: the ending shows the case once. The genitive after an article (always -en) and with none (-en with a noun's -s, -er for die and the plural); the plural; alle against viele; two adjectives. The gap text is Stefan's letter to Marko; the test is the Kirchtag.
- **schnellere**: -er or -st- and then the ending; the umlaut stays; mehr and weniger take none; no am before a noun; am besten, lieber, am liebsten; immer + the comparative, immer mehr + a noun; je … desto, umso. The gap text is Franz comparing everything; the test is Stefan's bigger cellar.
- **um-zu**: zu last after a verb, a noun with haben, es ist with an adjective; none after a modal; zu inside a separable verb, apart after be- and -ieren; um … zu, never für; damit for another person, right for one too; ohne … zu, statt … zu. The gap text is Stefan's morning; the test is Florian going up.
- **obwohl-trotzdem**: obwohl with the verb last, and first in the sentence; trotzdem in first place or after the verb; which goes with which fact; against weil and deshalb; aber and trotz. The gap text is the walk up the Dobratsch; the test is Tante Mitzi's radio.

**Only what came before.** The units assume A1, A2 and B1's units 1–6 (the Präteritum, als, the Plusquamperfekt, the genitive and its prepositions), as the outline orders them. The genitive's page is part a's, so `adjektiv-alle-faelle` names none and says what it needs in its step; `trotz des Regens` is said on the spot in `obwohl-trotzdem`. The sentences around the gaps stay in the present and the Perfekt.

**From the integrated A2.** The outline's "once merged, also" column for this part is done: `adjektiv-der`, `adjektiv-ein`, `adjektiv-null` on `adjektiv-alle-faelle`, `steigerung` on `schnellere`, `konnektoren` on `obwohl-trotzdem`, as their pages exist in the base. `verben-praepositionen` on `relativsatz-praeposition` and `trennbare-verben` on `um-zu` are added beside the outline's list, since the units use them.

**Title against the mistake.** `Ich habe Lust zu kochen` needs no comma: a bare infinitive after a noun may go without ([§ 75, E1](https://www.rechtschreibrat.com/DOX/rfdr_Regeln_2016_redigiert_2018.pdf)). `infinitiv-zu` says what the rule says.

## Grading (grading/de.json)

Nine paired rules appended, each only for a word of its group; `GermanB1bContentTest` tests both sides.

- **denn** or **deren** for denen, and denen for deren. Before, each was a slip in the stem.
- The umlaut left out of a comparative or superlative **with its ending**: altere for ältere, großeren for größeren, hohere and hochere for höhere, jungste for jüngste, altester for ältester. A2's rule stopped at the bare -er and -sten.
- A separable verb's zu-infinitive without its **zu**, or with one where none goes: aufstehen for aufzustehen, anrufen for anzurufen, aufzustehen for aufstehen. It was forgiven as two letters in the stem.
- **Stadt** for statt and the other way.

An ending needs no rule: `schnellerer` for `schnelleren` is wrong already. `dem` for `den` and the like are under four letters. Punctuation doesn't count, so a comma is never graded: the sheets write it, the page explains it.

**Spoken** (right and labelled, never a distractor or a wrong option): `der Tag, wo`; `obwohl` with the verb second; `trotzdem` as a conjunction; `ohne was zu sagen`. So no choice in `obwohl-trotzdem` offers `Trotzdem es regnet` or `obwohl er hat`, a choice on it offers `Weil` or `deshalb`, and no quick question has `obwohl` for its typed answer.

**Accepted as right** (`accept`): `welcher, welche, welches` for der, die, das on every typed pronoun; `umso` for desto; `anstatt` for statt; `auch wenn`, `da`, `daher`, `also`, `doch` and `dennoch` where they fit a gap; `Ohne` beside `Statt`, and `statt` and `um` beside `ohne`, where the sentence takes them; the adjective on the other noun (`das alte Haus des Müllers`); `desto müder wird er` beside `ist er`; `arbeitet aber trotzdem`.
