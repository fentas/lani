## For the native check

Austrian German first. **The first three are questions a dictionary didn't settle.**

1. **wann for wenn** (`dass-wenn-ob`). The sheets accept it as spoken, with wenn as the standard, and step 2 says "in some regions
   you'll also hear wann". Is it said in Carinthia, and in speech a learner would meet, or only in dialect? If not, take it off:
   the `spoken` on two gaps and two transform lines, and that sentence. Sources: [DWDS](https://www.dwds.de/wb/wann) has the
   conjunction as *veraltet*, [de.wiktionary](https://de.wiktionary.org/wiki/wann) only a maintenance banner ("landschaftliche
   Bedeutung im Sinne von 'wenn'/'als'", no region). A search found no Duden or Austrian dictionary entry, so nothing here settles it.
2. **älter wie for älter als** (`groesser-als`), spoken and accepted on every als. From a search extract of
   [Kubczak, Größer als oder größer wie?](https://ids-pub.bsz-bw.de/frontdoor/index/index/year/2014/docId/2227): common in central and
   southern Germany, als in the north and in the norm. The PDF sits behind a bot check and **wasn't opened**; Austria isn't named there.
3. **weil with the verb second** (`weil-denn`), spoken and accepted on every weil sentence that differs, and in a gap before a
   verb-second clause. Search extracts only ([a term paper on Duden and Eisenberg](https://www.hausarbeiten.de/document/174297):
   Duden mostly rejects it as not correct). Taught as "people say it, write the verb last".

Bold is a page written here. What each unit teaches:

- **adjektiv-der**: no ending after sein; -e in five places, -en everywhere else; dieser, jeder, welcher the same; hoch → hohe, teuer → teure, dunkel → dunkle. The gap text is Kathi's kitchen, the test the Kirchtag.
- **adjektiv-ein**: -er and -es where the article has no ending; everywhere else as after der; kein and the possessives, with their plural -en; der neue Hut against ein neuer Hut. The gap text is Stanko's stall at the market, the test Stefan's new tractor.
- **adjektiv-null**: der's ending on the adjective, in each case; a plural, viele, einige, wenige and a number; Guten Morgen against Gute Nacht, Lieber Stefan. The gap text is Kathi's menu, the test Oma Resi's cooking.
- **groesser-als**: -er als; the umlaut, and the words with none; so … wie; am -sten with its e after -t, -ß, -z; gut, gern, viel. Only after sein or a verb: before a noun comes in B1. The gap text is Franz comparing everything, the test the children's race at the Kirchtag.
- **weil-denn**: und, aber, oder, denn and sondern take no place; deshalb (deswegen, darum accepted) takes first place; weil sends the verb last, with two verbs and a separable one; a weil clause first; Warum? Weil …. The gap text is Mitzi on why Stefan didn't come, the test Kathi's Ruhetag.
- **dass-wenn-ob**: dass after glauben, wissen, hoffen, denken, sagen, and that glauben may drop it; wenn with wann set apart; ob against wenn, which English and Italian say with one word; a question word kept, the verb last; a separable verb whole at the end. The gap text is what Mitzi knows, the test the Almabtrieb.

**Only what came before around a gap**: the units assume all of A1 and A2's units 1–12 (the dative, the two-way prepositions, the modal verbs, the Perfekt, reflexives), as the outline orders them, whether or not they are merged yet. `adjektiv-der` has no adjective after ein, the comparison none before a noun.

## The spoken forms

A spoken form is never wrong (COURSE.md "Spoken forms"), so none of these three is ever a choice's wrong option or a bank's distractor. A choice about the word order offers denn's and deshalb's own mistakes (`Denn er krank ist`, `Deshalb er krank ist`, `sie geht` after deshalb), never `weil er hat`. A choice after a comparative never offers `wie`; `so … als` is the wrong option where so asks for wie. A quick question can't take `spoken` (a cloze has no such key), so none has als or wenn for its answer.

## The new pages

`adjektiv-der`, `adjektiv-ein`, `adjektiv-null`, `steigerung` and `konnektoren`, all A2, in the format of the book's German pages: the rule, the table, examples with notes, a hand-written card with a tip. `dass-wenn-ob` writes no page: it names the book's `nebensatz`, `fragen` and `wortstellung`. The intro tables of the three adjective units have article and adjective in each cell (der große, den großen), the noun in the head: the print marks an ending only in a cell of two words at most. The pages' tables have the whole phrase.

## Grading (grading/de.json)

Five paired rules, each only for a word of its group, so a slip on any other word stays almost:

- the comparative's and the superlative's umlaut left out (alter for älter, kalter for kälter, grosser for größer, altesten for ältesten), and hoher or hocher for höher;
- the other way, the umlaut where none goes (größer for großer, älter for alter);
- hoche for hohe; größesten for größten;
- denn, dann, wenn and wann for each other (one letter apart, so they used to pass as slips).

An adjective's ending needs no rule: the app already takes it as the ending, never a slip. das and dass are wrong both ways already. `teuere` for teure stays almost, on purpose: it is rare, not clearly wrong.
