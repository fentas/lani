## For the native check

Austrian German first. **The first eight are questions a dictionary didn't settle.**

1. **Auch wenn es regnet, wir gehen** (`zwar-aber`), `spoken` on the lines where a fronted auch wenn or selbst wenn is natural, and named in step 3 and on the page. From memory; no source looked at. The standard's verb-second main clause is the shown form. If nobody grown up says it in Carinthia, take the `spoken` off and the sentence with it.
2. **dir's, ihm's, ihr's** (`satzbau`), es hanging on a dative pronoun: `spoken`, in step 2 and on the page. The search for a source found none. `dir es` written whole isn't said; a choice therefore never offers an es-order: the wrong orders offered are `ihm ihn`.
3. **der Stefan, die Oma, die Kathi** (`satzbau`), `spoken` on the lines where a name begins the answer. The article before a first name is the regional norm in Bavarian and Alemannic dialects, by a [paper on Austria (Ziegler, Lenzhofer, ide 2018)](https://ide.aau.at/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/2018-4-Ziegler-Lenzhofer.pdf), search extract only.
4. **jedoch with no place** (`zwar-aber`): "after a comma jedoch may also take no place". From memory of Duden; [Duden's page](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/jedoch) wouldn't open (404). The sheets accept `jedoch` where aber stands, and the bank never offers it as wrong.
5. **dadurch, dass with its comma** (`indem-sodass`). Written with a comma throughout. A search found no source that settles `dadurch dass` as one conjunction, and the outline writes it so. The grader ignores commas, so nothing hangs on it but the page.
6. **Frau Magister for a woman** (`formeller-brief`). The [Veritas school sheet](https://www.scook.at/cdn_proxy/a74311b6-8ae0-4ba7-8548-e9a428b40afe/0525900101_0531670101_AB_029_Anrede_und_Titel_AB.pdf) has Magistra and Mag.a; [karriere.at](https://www.karriere.at/c/a/titel-oesterreich) opens with "Grüß Gott Frau Magistra". The outline's title says Frau Magister; from memory, that's what's said. Every form is accepted: Frau Magister, Frau Magistra, Frau Mag., Frau Mag.a, each with the name or without, and the same for Dr.in. A native eye on which the page should lead with.
7. **in der Arbeit, zu Ostern** (`oesterreichisch`): [Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterreichisches_Deutsch) gives both, and says "in der Arbeit" is southern German too. Named on the page only.
8. **Carinthian sentences made up from sourced words** (`oesterreichisch`): *Ich komme lei morgen*, *Das ist nit letz*, *Das ist nit teuer*, *Hast du eppes gehört?*, *Die Tür ist ofn*, *Ich bin miad*, *Ich hab lei zwei Euro*. The words are sourced (see Sources); the sentences aren't: a real speaker would turn more than one word to dialect (*i hob lei zwa Euro*). On a sheet each is right and labelled; the unit says it only asks you to understand.

Then the sentences, for naturalness:

- **indem-sodass**: "Franz rettete sein Mehl, indem er die Säcke nach oben trug"; "Toni half ihm, ohne dass man ihn bitten musste"; "Dadurch, dass alle mithalfen, war die Arbeit am Abend fertig"; "Und anstatt dass Franz Geld zahlte, gab es für alle eine Jause"; "Der Bach ist jetzt zu tief, um zu Fuß durchzugehen".
- **zwar-aber**: "Heuer war der Kirchtag zwar kurz, aber schön"; "Kathi verdiente gut, wohingegen Mitzi wenig verkaufte"; "Der alte läuft noch, allerdings ist er laut"; "Einerseits braucht er ihn, andererseits fehlt ihm das Geld".
- **nomen-verb**: "Franz stellt einen Antrag auf mehr Geld"; "Das ganze Dorf nimmt Abschied von …"; "Ich gebe euch morgen Bescheid"; a Bürgermeisterin in the hall; each plain verb in the page's table (entscheiden, informieren, bezweifeln, da sein, berücksichtigen).
- **satzbau**: every order a reorder accepts, above all "Gestern hat mir im Garten Oma geholfen", "Dem Kind gibt ihn Stefan", "Die Suppe bringt mir Kathi heute"; "Dann setzt sich Stefan auf die Bank"; the name *te-ka-mo-lo*.
- **formeller-brief**: both letters, and the claim that an exclamation mark after the opening is common in Austria (the Veritas sheet has one each way); "Der Weg zu meinem Obstgarten ist seit dem Winter kaputt"; "Ich ersuche Sie, die anderen Kisten bis Freitag zu liefern"; `Beilage`.
- **oesterreichisch**: every pair of words; "Das geht sich nicht aus"; "Ich habe auf deinen Geburtstag vergessen"; "Das Sackerl kostet zehn Cent. Ich kaufe es um zehn Cent"; "Oma Resi ist am Ofen gesessen"; "Toni, heuer mache ich die Knödel selbst"; that Jause has "no one word in Germany".
- **The six pages**: each rule and each card's tip.

Then each base:

- **Slovene**: tako, da for indem; s tem, da for dadurch, dass; "Spoštovana gospa Huber", "Spoštovani," for Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren, "S spoštovanjem"; "dati predlog", "vložiti vlogo", "sprejeti sklep", "nuditi pomoč"; "malica" for Jause, and that it came from južina; "sicer …, toda"; "govorjena koroška nemščina" for spoken Carinthian (koroščina alone reads as the Slovene dialect). The masculine default where the learner speaks (Rad bi se pritožil, Hvaležen bi vam bil).
- **Italian**: the gerund for indem (vendendo); "invece che" for anstatt dass; "Gentile signora Huber", "Gentili signore e signori", "Cordiali saluti", "Allegato"; "presentare una domanda", "prendere una delibera", "prestare aiuto"; "merenda" for Jause; "sì …, ma"; "il carinziano parlato". Lei and its capitals in the letters.
- **English**: British usage (Dear Ms Huber, Yours sincerely, "Dear Sir or Madam", "cider").

Every explanation, why and translation in en, sl and it is machine-written. Each file's `review` field names its own check items.

What each unit teaches:

- **indem-sodass**: indem and dadurch, dass for how; sodass and so … dass for what came of it, against B1's damit; zu …, um … zu; ohne dass and anstatt dass with a second person, against B1's ohne … zu. The gap text is the water at Franz's mill, the test the way to the Alm.
- **zwar-aber**: zwar … aber; jedoch, allerdings, dennoch; auch wenn, selbst wenn against obwohl; während and wohingegen for whereas, während's third job; einerseits … andererseits; the three kinds of word order sorted; und zwar named apart. The gap text is the Kirchtag in the rain, the test Stefan's new tractor.
- **nomen-verb**: the idea of a pair and its plain verb; stellen; zur Verfügung stehen against stellen; nehmen; Bescheid and the others; the bracket; kein against nicht with a pair. The gap text is the meeting in the village hall, the test the fountain on the square.
- **satzbau**: pronouns first; accusative before dative with two pronouns, dative before accusative with two nouns; time, cause, manner, place as the usual order; first place; nicht's place; nicht before one word, with sondern. Six reorders, each taking every natural order. The gap text is Stefan bringing the cider, the test Kathi's soup.
- **formeller-brief**: the opening; Austrian titles; Betreff, the close, Beilage; a request; a complaint; dabei, dafür, diese, daher. The gap text is Stefan's letter to the council, the test Kathi's complaint to a brewery.
- **oesterreichisch**: the year; the kitchen; the house and the day; ich bin gesessen; um, auf Urlaub, auf etwas vergessen, es geht sich aus, das Cola; spoken Carinthian to understand. The gap text is Oma Resi's note, the test at Kathi's.

**Only what came before around a gap**: all of A1 to B1, and B2's units 1–12 as the outline orders them, though none of their pages is named. No sentence needs one.

## Austria's word and Germany's

Never a wrong answer, never a wrong option. **Two `accept`s**, the Austrian first: every gap and cell of `oesterreichisch` asking for a word, every mix on a whole line (`Wir fahren heuer im Januar in den Urlaub`), ist and hat before gesessen. `GermanB2cContentTest` grades twelve of the pairs and the mixes. A choice about a word asks what it means (`Hol mir zwei Paradeiser! Was holst du?` Tomaten), so Germany's word stands there as the right meaning, and the wrong options are other things.

So a cell asking "in Carinthia" takes Tomate too, silently: the page and the sort say which is which. An item's `spoken` would have shown the Austrian form but calls Germany's standard word spoken, and it isn't.

## The spoken forms

Never a choice's wrong option or a bank's distractor. All are `spoken`, right and labelled, beside the unit's point: *Auch wenn es regnet, wir gehen*; *Selbst wenn Toni keine Zeit hat, er kommt*; *dir's*, *ihm's*, *ihr's*; *der Stefan*, *die Kathi*; *wegen dem Regen*; lei, nit, eppes, ofn, miad and *was* for etwas in `oesterreichisch`'s lines.

The two lists in `oesterreichisch`'s sheet 3 write Carinthian into the standard, as Slovene's `govorjeni-jezik` writes spoken Slovene; the spoken line is right and labelled, the correction the standard.

## Grading (grading/de.json)

Every right answer of the six units is graded right. New in `same`, one answer whatever the spelling, each from Duden: so dass and sodass, in Frage and infrage, anderseits, andrerseits and andererseits. New in `wrong`, a word of its own and no slip, each only for its own word: war for zwar; teuer, neuer, Feuer and heuer; Fieber and Feber; Pause and Jause; Tropfen and Topfen; Kessel and Sessel; Kosten, Karten, Kisten and Kasten; Männer and Jänner; biete and bitte; versuche and ersuche; stehle for stelle; sehe for stehe; lasse, passe for fasse. The note is its own key, `_b2c_note`, so that the other parts' additions to `_note` can't collide with it. No `nonstandard` is added: a spoken form here is beside the point, or the unit's point is reading it.

`in dem` for indem and `das` for dass were wrong already, by the word count and the ending.
