### The grading (grading/en.json)

Appended, for the units; `EnglishA2cContentTest` holds each:
- `same`, placed before the rule that reads he's as he is, so **order matters: check it after a merge**: 's before going to is is
  (Jack's going to); 's before a participle is has where be can't stand (she's seen, Jack's never been), before one that could be an
  adjective or a passive (done, made, lost, closed, written …) only with just, already, never, ever or an object after it (she's
  lost her key; it's written stays it is). So "she is seen" and "he is gone" aren't taken for it. I'd love, prefer, hate, enjoy.
- `loose`, a contraction typed without its apostrophe is almost: hes seen, id like, and ill help, well go only before another word,
  so the words themselves stay words.
- `wrong`, paired both ways, one letter apart and else forgiven as a slip: never and ever, done and gone, seen and been, could and
  would, went and want, then and when; base form, past and participle where they differ by a letter (came and come, gave and give,
  made and make, wrote and write, met for meet, swam and swum, sang and sung, drank and drunk, began and begun).

## How the units are written

As COURSE.md "Writing a unit" and "The English course", plus what these six found (for COURSE.md's section, once part c has integrated):
- **A bracketed translation is one sentence** ("(Die Tasche ist schwer, ich trage sie.)"): the sheet's rule line splits at a sentence
  end even inside brackets, so "(Die Tasche ist schwer. Ich trage sie.)" printed cut.
- **A gap takes all that is right**, not only what the unit teaches: "___ I close the window?" takes Shall, Should, Can, Could, May;
  "You look tired. You ___ rest." takes should, must, have to, need to, ought to; "I think it ___ snow" takes will and is going to.
- **The plain present isn't always wrong**: a timetable's "The bus leaves at nine" is right, so it isn't offered as a mistake, and the
  colloquial "It's raining tomorrow" isn't either.
- **A choice's `almost`** for what is right but off the point: "I'm going to" where the phone is ringing, "I'll" for what was decided before.
- **'s + a participle** is has in the grading, listed once; a name's 's before -ing still lists its long form beside it.
