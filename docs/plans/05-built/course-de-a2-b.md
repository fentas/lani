## For the native check

Austrian German first:

- **partizip-perfekt**: ich bin gesessen, gestanden, gelegen as the page and the intro teach them, and "Northern Germany says
  hat gesessen" (Duden says *sein* is southern German, Austrian and Swiss; the Austrian sheet says the same; see Sources).
  Das Bild ist gehangen (hat gehangen accepted) against hat gehängt. zusperren and zugesperrt ("Kathi hat um zehn
  zugesperrt"). Der Bauer aus Arnoldstein.
- **muessen-duerfen**: Magst du einen Most? as the Austrian offer; "Du sollst nur fahren" (and whether sollen is right there); Rückenschmerzen as an everyday word
  beside "Kreuzweh".
- **war-hatte**: the claim that Austria often says ich bin gewesen where the page says ich war; "Maxi durfte bis zehn bleiben";
  Kirchtag in Arnoldstein.
- **reflexiv**: sich verkühlen (Duden: "besonders österreichisch") as the village's word; "Das geht sich aus" as an Austrian
  favourite and how it's glossed; the order "Heute freut sich Kathi" (both "Am Sonntag freut Kathi sich" and "… sich Kathi" are
  accepted); "Wir treffen uns um acht"; "Maxi, beeil dich!"; the Perfekt "Stefan hat sich verkühlt".
- **warten-auf**: the choice of chunks (warten auf, aufpassen auf, denken an, schreiben an, sich freuen auf and über, sprechen mit
  and über, Angst haben vor, erzählen von, träumen von; the page adds lachen über, bitten um, sich interessieren für, fragen
  nach); "Lena schreibt an die Oma"; "Jakob hat Angst vor dem Krampus" (an adult course's joke, not the child's Italian course).
- **am-ersten-mai**: Jänner, Feber (Duden: "österreichisch, besonders Amtssprache"), heuer; der erste Fünfte and der sechste Erste as
  a date read out; "Der Wievielte ist heute?"; im dritten Stock; siebte beside siebente. The holidays named are 1 May, 6 January
  and 26 October, from memory, not from a source.

Each base, by the same reader:

- **Slovene**: the aspects and the first person's gender in translations ("Včeraj nisem mogel priti", "Rad bi kavo", the unit's
  speaker has no gender of its own); the grammar words (deležnik, povratni glagol, vrstilni števnik, kratki preteklik); zveza for a chunk of verb and preposition; "se" and
  "si" for mich and mir; Podklošter for Arnoldstein, Videm for Udine.
- **Italian**: "sich freuen auf: non vedere l'ora di"; "bastano giusti giusti" for Das geht sich aus; the passato breve; "non sei

- **English**: mustn't against don't have to, "a bad back" for Rückenschmerzen; British first.

What each unit teaches:

- **muessen-duerfen**: müssen, dürfen, wollen, sollen and mögen beside A1's können and möchten, with ich and er alike; the other verb
  at the very end; ich will is I want; sollen is what someone else wants; mögen with a noun and gern with a verb, and the Austrian
  Magst du einen Most?; the trap, nicht müssen against nicht dürfen, with each base's own way of falling in it. The gap text is
  Kathi's day off; the test is Florian's morning on the Alm.
- **partizip-perfekt**: every kind of participle: ge-…-t, -et, the mixed ones (gebracht, gewusst), ge-…-en with and without a new
  vowel, ge in the middle of a separable verb (and zugesperrt), none with be-, ver-, er-, ent-, -ieren (bekommen → bekommen);
  haben or sein, with the change of state (aufstehen, einschlafen) and sein, bleiben, passieren; part a's stehen and stellen in the
  Perfekt, which its outline line left for this unit: ist gesessen, gestanden, gelegen against hat gestellt. The gap text is
  Stefan's Monday; the test is Tante Mitzi's lost bag.
- **war-hatte**: war, hatte, and the modals' -te with no umlaut, with hätte and könnte set against them; es gab, always singular;
  how people mix them with the Perfekt, and that ich bin gewesen is as right. The gap text is the Kirchtag in Arnoldstein; the test is
  Opa Sepp's old days.
- **reflexiv**: mich, dich, sich, uns, euch, sich; the verbs that come with it, with the Austrian sich verkühlen (sich erkälten
  named); its place (Heute freut sich Kathi); Perfekt with haben; mir and dir when the verb has another object, as Slovene si (Italian keeps mi);
  Setz dich!, Setzen Sie sich!; wir treffen uns; Das geht sich aus. The gap text is the morning at Kathi's; the test is Tante Mitzi's
  cold. The Slovene and Italian explanations say what each language shares with it.
- **warten-auf**: each chunk with its case: warten auf and aufpassen auf; denken an, schreiben an; sich freuen auf (ahead) and über
  (here); sprechen mit (the person) and über (the topic); Angst haben vor, erzählen von, träumen von; pronouns for people (auf dich,
  mit mir, vor ihm). The gap text is Villach station; the test is Lena's birthday. No woran or darauf: those are B1's `woran-daran`.
- **am-ersten-mai**: -te, -ste and the four special ones; the date with der and with am; vom … bis zum, im dritten Stock, zum ersten
  Mal; vor zwei Tagen and in einer Woche, with the dative's -n; Jänner, Feber and heuer with Januar, Februar and dieses Jahr accepted.
  The gap text is Kathi's calendar; the test is Stefan's birthday.

Around their gaps the units use only what comes before them in the outline: A1, part a's dative, its prepositions and its two-way
prepositions, and this part's earlier units. No attributive adjective with an ending of its own (part c's), no clause with the verb last
(part c's). The ordinals' -e and -en are named as such, in fixed frames (der erste, am ersten), which part c's `adjektiv-der` then
explains.

## The grammar pages

Five new A2 pages, each its unit's first page and summary card, each with a hand-written card: `partizip-zwei`, `praeteritum-kurz`,
`reflexive-verben`, `verben-praepositionen`, `ordinalzahlen`. A rule of 1,050 to 1,200 characters per base (the format allows 1,200), a
table, 8 to 10 examples with notes. Each English rule says its mistakes as "(never *…*)" for the print's "Achtung". They name only
existing pages and each other under `see`; the lead's "once merged" pass can add `pronomen-dativ` to `reflexiv` as the outline's table
says.

## Grading (grading/de.json)

Every right answer of the six units is graded right. Paired rules only, each only for a word of its group, a slip elsewhere forgiven;
`GermanA2bContentTest` tests both sides. New in `wrong`:

- A participle typed for another one a letter away: gesessen, gegessen; gekommen, bekommen, genommen; gelesen, gewesen, gelegen;
  gesucht, besucht; gemacht, gelacht; gefunden, gebunden; gestellt, bestellt. Before, each was forgiven as a slip in the stem.
- A mixed verb's participle with the infinitive's vowel: gewisst, gekennt, genennt, gebrennt, gerennt.
- A modal's plural vowel on the singular (ich konn, du wollst) and müsst against musst, either way: ihr and du, not a slip.
- The short past's vowel: kannte, darfte, willte for konnte, durfte, wollte.
- könnte, müsste, dürfte, hätte against konnte, musste, durfte, hatte, and wäre, wärst, wären against war, warst, waren, either way:
  with the umlaut typed it was almost before, for the very thing the unit teaches.
- -te for an ordinal's -ste (zwanzigte, dreißigte). neunzehnste and achtte were wrong already, by the ending.
- auch for euch.

`ich hab` stays right, as `same` has it. Choices never grade the German-German form wrong: hat gesessen is asked in a `multi` where both are
right, typed lists take both.
