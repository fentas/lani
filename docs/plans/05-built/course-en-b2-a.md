## How the units are written

As COURSE.md "Writing a unit" and "The English course", plus what these six found (for COURSE.md's section, once part c has integrated):

- **The print's Watch out reads English's say.** The older pages a unit also names come next, so `past-simple`'s I winned! took a place on narrative-tenses' sheet. Two or three `**R** (not *W*)` in the English `say` put the unit's own mistakes there. Only the English needs them.
- **`been` is almost for have been, so it reaches a wrong option:** It might been the wind was offered as wrong, and the test caught it. It might be been replaced it.
- **A gap with by + a time also takes will + the base form** (I'll finish it by Friday), so frames that want the perfect say already, by the time or for ten years.
- **`spoken` lists what is said in full, line by line**, and the table's row is the net for a line that doesn't. A cloze has no `spoken`, so what's said goes in its `accept`.
- **`{m:|f:a}` doesn't suit every word.** Tekel + a gives Tekela: the whole pair, {m:Tekel|f:Tekla}. `{m:|a}` is refused.
- **Both needn't have and didn't need to read alike in a base unless you make them differ.** Slovene's "Ni mi bilo treba" was both until the reader said so.
- **Punctuation inside an answer is ignored** (`Grading.normalize`), so a colon, a comma or a full stop between two sentences needs no second answer. The English reader, told otherwise by my brief, asked for them; not needed.
