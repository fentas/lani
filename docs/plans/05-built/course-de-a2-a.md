# Built: the German school's first six A2 units (2026-11-02)

## For the native check (first)

All of it is machine-written (Claude): six units, four grammar pages, the lexicon's new entries, in German, English, Slovene and
Italian. Nothing has been read by a native speaker, and every file says so in its `review`. Start with what no source settled:

- **Austrian German, a spoken form accepted on one general source only.** `am Platz` and `am Sessel` for auf dem Platz and auf dem
  Sessel. The source (below) gives am for auf dem as colloquial and names am Berg, am Opernball and am Tisch, not these two. They
  are `spoken` forms: right and labelled, never a mistake.
- **Austrian German, no source looked for.** `seit der Früh` (intro table of `mit-nach-bei`), `das Sackerl` in `aus dem Sackerl`, `Kathi
  deckt den Tisch`, `Lena räumt auf`, `Die Gläser stehen`, `Das Messer liegt`, and whether a Carinthian says `Ich danke Ihnen` to a guest.
- **German, the sentences a learner will copy.** `Dem Hund gebe ich Wasser, der Katze Milch.`, `Stanko gibt den Bienen Zucker.`, `Kathi
  bringt sie ihm.`, `Toni zeigt ihn uns.`, `Bello läuft im Garten` against `in den Garten`, `Maxi fährt mit dem Rad gegen den Wind.`
- **What the person-last orders accept.** The transforms of `wem-dativ` accept `Kathi bringt die Suppe dem Gast.` beside `Kathi bringt
  dem Gast die Suppe.`, as grammatical with stress on the person. If that reads wrong, take them out of `accept`.
- **The upright and flat rule** (`stehen-stellen`): an upright glass stands and a flat book lies, and `Die Gläser stehen` and `Die Messer liegen`
  follow from it. A bottle on its side lies, so the sheets only ask this where the sentence decides.
- **Slovene.** `tekniti` for schmecken; `gospa` for Frau where the German means a woman at the inn; `posaditi` for setzen; `k jezeru` for
  an den See and zum See; the dual in the kitchen's gap text (`vajina`, `prideta`, `jima`), for Maxi and Jakob; `v Beljak` for nach Villach.
- **Italian.** `stato` and `moto` as the words for wo? and wohin?; `da` for both bei and zu, which step 4 of `mit-nach-bei` says outright;
  `Il miele è Suo?`; `in cucina` for both in der Küche and in die Küche.
- **English.** `alpine pasture` for die Alm, `lime tree` for die Linde, `Kathi lays the table`.

**The size** is a little above Italian A2's 27 to 30 items a unit, because a sort of two or three columns counts each word as an item. Slovene's A2
units are of the same shape.

**The word order of the gaps' surroundings.** Each unit uses only what comes before it. Around a gap: no modal but möchten and können, no
adjective with an ending, no weil or denn, no reflexive beyond the chunk Setz dich! and Ich setze mich (taught as chunks in unit 6, the rule in
unit 10). Prepositions before their unit are fixed chunks (im Gasthaus, in die Küche, auf der Alm). No weak noun takes the dative here: Herr,
Bub, Bauer, Hirte and Nachbar are left out, since `herr-kollege` comes in B2. Herr Rossi only ever stands as the subject.

## The grammar pages

Four new A2 pages in the format of A1's, each with a hand-written card and a tip. Each is its unit's first page, except where the outline names an
existing one first:

- **`pronomen-dativ`**: ich, mich, mir and the rest in a table; the verb decides; helfen, danken, gefallen, schmecken, gehören; es geht mir gut; the order of two pronouns.
- **`praepositionen-dativ`**: the seven, what each is for, zum, zur, beim, vom; wo? bei, wohin? zu, woher? von; nach Hause, zu Hause, daheim.
- **`praepositionen-akkusativ`**: the six; only a der noun shows it; pronouns; bis zum, where zu decides; set against the dative's list.
- **`stehen-stellen`**: the three pairs and hängen; upright and flat; the present; der Sessel and der Kasten.

Part a names only existing pages and its own. Nothing in its pages' `see` points to another part's page. `dativ`, `faelle`, `akkusativ` and
`wechselpraepositionen` are named as they are, and not rewritten; each unit has its own `intro.summary`, so a summary card shows the unit's own words.

## Grading (grading/de.json)

Twelve paired rules are appended to `wrong`. Each is only for the word of its group, so a keyboard's slip on any other word stays almost. They are needed because a
one-letter difference in a stem is otherwise forgiven:

- legen and liegen, in the forms ich, du and er and the infinitive, for each other (legt for liegt);
- setzen and sitzen for each other (setzt for sitzt);
- ihren, ihrem, ihrer, ihres for ihnen, and the other way;
- auch for euch and the other way; noch for nach and the other way;
- geben or gehen for gegen; ich hilfe for helfe.

The endings needed no new rule: dem for den, zum for zur and the like are under four letters, where no slip is forgiven, and einem for einen was already in the mein, dein, ein group.
The sentence in `_note` is in the middle of the note, so that the other parts' appended sentences don't overlap it in `json3merge.py`.

Every long form is accepted beside its contraction (zu dem Arzt beside zum Arzt, in das beside ins).

## The words (lexicon/de.extra.json)

file now has two entries each for liegen and aufräumen, which the lookup reads as one (`Lexicon.readings` checked for liegst, liegt, räumt, aufgeräumt).

The course's units aren't in that count; `school-prints --explain` lists every word of the six units with a meaning in each base (none left off), and a script of this round found every German word of the units in the supplement, the packs or the cast.

**One thing to know:** `gefällt` has two readings, fällen's participle (to fell a tree, earlier in the file) and gefallen. The lookup ranks by the line's translation, and the gloss of gefallen has "like" in it for that reason.
