## Settled from sources

Regions (raccontare), said plainly and mildly in the unit, and on the page:

- The North says the prossimo in speech, and the local speech there often has no remoto: [Treccani, passato prossimo](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/passato-prossimo_(Enciclopedia-dell'Italiano)/).
- The South uses the remoto where the standard has the prossimo: the same article; clearly preferred in the Sicilian, Calabrian and Salentine area:
  [Treccani, passato remoto](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/passato-remoto_(Enciclopedia-dell'Italiano)/). The fetched text of that article also has one sentence, "al Nord una netta preferenza per il passato remoto" for
  personal narration, which contradicts its neighbours and every other source. It wasn't used.
- **Tuscany is the weakest claim**: the first article names it as the exception to the prossimo's spread; "both, by distance" otherwise came through a forum quoting Crusca. The unit says "you hear both, by distance".
- -ere verbs' two forms (vendei, vendetti), from the second article, applied to credere; diede and dette from general grammar, not read on a page.

History, checked on Wikipedia's pages: Aquileia founded in 181 BC, capital of Augustus's tenth region, the end of the amber trade ([Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquileia)); Marco Polo left in 1271 and came back in 1295
([Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo)); Galileo saw Jupiter's moons in December 1609 or January 1610, the line says 1610 ([Galilean moons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_moons)); Dante was born in Florence around 1265
([Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri)). The mosaic floor with its fish: the repository's own reading `i-pesci-di-aquileia`, Wikipedia's page names the mosaics and not the fish.

Gerund (one subject, the reason, pur + the gerund): only teaching sites, which agree: [elon.io, cause](https://elon.io/grammar/italian/verbs/gerund/cause-reason), [elon.io, pur](https://elon.io/grammar/italian/complex/concession-pur-gerundio),
[Think in Italian](https://www.thinkinitalian.com/italian-past-gerund/). They disagree on whether essendo always gives a cause, so the unit doesn't teach that it does.

## Writing rules these units follow

- **Only the outline's order around a gap**, part a's units assumed. Unit 10 uses only the remote pasts B1 taught, plus chiese and rispose with their meaning shown.
- **No gap where another tense is also Italian, unless every right form is listed.** Every shift gap of disse-che has a past anchor (quel giorno, l'anno dopo, il giorno dopo), so the present and verrà aren't graded wrong where they'd be right;
  verrà is taught and never asked. Raccontare's gaps name their tense.
- **A spoken form is never wrong, and never an option**: se lo sapevo, venivo; ha detto che veniva; mi chiese dov'era: in `spoken`, right and labelled, each mix a line can have listed whole. dasse and fossimo venuti, regional speech, were taken off
  the choices at the second read. A remoto for today is never graded wrong: it's how the South talks, so no choice offers andai against sono andato for stamattina.
- **No wrong option that is right Italian in its sentence**: partiva for Marco Polo (the history books' imperfetto narrativo), Verremmo, se abbiamo tempo, Matteo's se non dovessi guidare (he might be talking to someone), pioveva in a sort where it fits both columns: all gone at the second read.
  se-potessi's se vinco is `almost`: right Italian, but not what the prompt says.
- **Several right answers listed**: both orders of an if-sentence, the subject in either half or left out, l'avessi and lo avessi, disse and ha detto, venire or andare, avendo visto and veduto, ora or adesso left out.
- **A table's cell short**, as English A2 found: a phone's field holds about nine letters in three columns, twelve in two, eighteen in one. The past conditional's table asks only for the helper (… mangiato, … venuto).
- **Read aloud**: every bold form is one the intro shows or the unit's `words` has, so Elena reads it (a scratch check per base); quoted speech is bold too, or the phone's German voice would say «Sono stanco».
- **The print's "Watch out"**: the sheet reads the English text, and `**R** (never *W*)` pairs cleanly: se potrei against se potessi, tornerebbe against sarebbe tornato.
