- **New in `nonstandard`, almost with the standard form shown:**

### The six new pages (companion/grammar/en)

Each B1, in the format of A2's, with a hand-written card: `relative-clauses`, `passive`, `verb-patterns`, `phrasal-verbs`, `too-enough`, `reflexive-pronouns`. The rules run 880 to 1,170 characters per base, against the bridge's 1,200. The tip differs by base where the trap does: the comma and *ga, jo* (sl), der, die, das (de), *che* that can't drop (it); *wird* against *will*, *von*, *da*; *Hočem, da greš*, *Ich will, dass du gehst*, *Voglio che tu vada*; *aufstehen*, *buttare via*; the order of *dovolj toplo*, *abbastanza caldo*; *se, sich, si* for two things in English or for none.

## How the units are written

As COURSE.md "Writing a unit" and "The English course", plus what these six found (for COURSE.md's section, once part c has integrated):

- **The Watch out box reads English:** `**R** (never *W*)` pairs them whole. "**Turn it off.**, never *Turn off it*" printed ✓ turn off, because the box looked for a near bold form; the bracket fixed it.
- **A word on `words` prints its dictionary sense:** read the list as the sheet prints it. Six of mine meant something else.
- **A gap that teaches too still takes what's right:** "The soup is ___ hot: I can't eat it" takes too, so, very, really; "We haven't got ___ chairs!" takes enough, many, any. The choice in the intro, with wrong options nobody says, is where too alone gets tested.
- **A choice never offers was against were:** a typed gap asks it, and `was` is almost. Its wrong options are is make, was buy, were catch.
- **'s before a participle in a passive:** "It's made here" is it is, "It's seen" is it has by A2's rule. No line here asks the second, and no line asks a noun's 's (The bread's baked here), which the table doesn't read as is.
- **One sentence in a gap text is refused:** a gap text has 2 to 12 gaps.
