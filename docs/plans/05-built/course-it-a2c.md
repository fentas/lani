# Built: the Italian school's A2 units 13–18 (2026-10-31)

Teo (a child of about seven, learning Italian from German; the Friuli village on the coast, a tablet) has A1's 18
units, and A2 was outlined in `companion/course/it/course.json`: 18 units marked `"planned": true`. Three branches
write them side by side: `course-it-a2a` units 1–6 (riflessivi to superlativi), `course-it-a2b` units 7–12
(passato-essere to pronomi-diretti) and this branch, `course-it-a2c`, units 13–18 in the outline's order and with its
titles. The format and how a unit is written are companion/COURSE.md ("Writing a unit", "The Italian course"; this
part's summary is "The Italian course: A2, units 13–18").

## The units

Each has a guided intro of five or six steps (Maestra Elena's lines in German first, then Slovene and English, "du" in
German; every Italian form in bold for her voice with its meaning in brackets after it; two examples a step, the table
growing row by row, a quick question each), three worksheets from easy to harder (a sort or a table and choices, then
a gap text with a word bank, then transform lists) and a test at pass 80 with new sentences. The sentences are the
village's: Lorenzo's birthday, Davide's sheep Pallina and Bianca, Nonna Rosa at the market and in her kitchen, a night
in the tent with Moro, Zia Nives and her radio, Marisa's osteria and Tommaso (her son), the festa's ice cream, Bruno's
hot iron, a game of tag, the village's who's who and a test of riddles.

A1's units have 21–32 worksheet items and tests of 10–14: these are the same size.

- **pronomi-indiretti**: gli and le known from piace, now with dare, dire, portare (Gli do la palla); a + the person
  replaced (Do la palla a Lorenzo → Gli do la palla); mi, ti (Nonno, mi racconti una storia?); ci, vi and gli for a
  loro; Le to Maestra Elena and Signora Marisa (ti there is the choice's almost: right, the wrong register); lo or gli,
  with the two that differ from German: telefonare a takes le (Le telefono, though German says sie anrufen) and aiutare
  takes lo (Lo aiuto, though German says ihm helfen). The gap text: Lorenzo's birthday.
- **ci-ne**: ci for a place named (Vai al mare? Sì, ci vado), the ci of c'è; non ci; ne after a number (Ne ho sette,
  never ho sette alone); ne with an amount and the partitive (Vuoi del pane? Sì, ne voglio un po'), non ne ho; ce n'è
  and ce ne sono. The gap text: Nonna Rosa at the market.
- **negazioni**: non … mai (two words where German has one, as Slovene has), non … niente (nulla accepted everywhere),
  non … nessuno (people) against niente (things), non … più and non … ancora, nessuno first without non (Nessuno
  gioca con me), one-word answers. The gap text: a night in the tent.
- **vorrei**: vorrei against voglio (voglio is the choice's almost: right, but sharp), vorrei + a verb, vorresti,
  vorrebbe (lui, lei, Lei), vorremmo, vorreste, vorrebbero (two m in vorremmo, two r everywhere); potrei, potresti,
  potrebbe to the teacher (potresti there is almost); mi piacerebbe. The gap text: Marisa's osteria, with no distractors
  (every form of the conditional fits some person, so any would have been a trick).
- **imperativo-pronomi**: the pronoun on the end (guardami, aiutami, aspettami), lo, la, li, le and gli, le (prendila,
  mangialo, portagli); da', di', fa' doubling (dammi, dimmi, fallo) and gli single (dagli); noi and voi (aiutiamolo,
  ascoltatemi); the negative to one, non toccarlo or non lo toccare, both right, and to several. The gap text: Nonna
  Rosa's kitchen; the test's: a game of tag.
- **relative-che**: che joining two sentences, for animals and things and people; chi only the question; che never
  changing while the verb after it agrees with the noun before it (a sort: che gioca, che giocano); che as the object
  (il libro che leggo, la gubana che fa Nonna Rosa); never left out, the verb not at the end as in German. The gap
  text: who's who in the village; the test's: riddles (È un animale che fa il miele: l'ape!).

They assume A1 (all 18 units) and A2's units before them in the outline's order, which the other two parts write: the
reflexives, the modals (posso, puoi: Mi potresti aiutare, Puoi chiudere la porta? → Potresti …), the partitive (del
pane, delle caramelle with ne), the comparatives and superlatives (il pezzo più grande, buonissima), the past with
essere (Lorenzo è caduto), pronomi-diretti's lo, la, li, le (Lo vedo against Gli do; prendilo). Nothing around a gap
comes from a unit after it: pronomi-indiretti says non ti dico il segreto, not non … niente (negazioni comes later).

## The grammar pages

Six new A2 pages in the Italian book (`companion/grammar/it/`), each the book's usual size (a rule per base of about
1,000 characters, a table of six to ten rows, seven or eight examples, notes on the ones that teach), in it, sl, en and
de: `pronomi-indiretti`, `ci-ne`, `negazioni`, `vorrei`, `imperativo-pronomi`, `relative-che`. The outline names them
(`grammar`), and each unit names its own page first (the summary card), then the earlier pages it builds on
(piacere; ce-ci-sono and partitivo; verbi-irregolari and tu-lei; imperativo and pronomi-indiretti). The pages go a
little further than the units where the book should (gli for loro also as a loro, no agreement with Le ho dato, ce
l'ho left out, nessun and nessuna, neanch'io, mi scusi and mi dica with Lei, cui named for later). A page's English
says its mistakes as "(never *…*)", which the print's "Achtung · Watch out" takes (below).

## Grading

- **Every right answer is graded right** by the Italian grading, and every other right answer is listed: niente and
  nulla; non toccarlo and non lo toccare (non toccarla and non la toccare in a gap); Non apritela, non la aprite, non
  l'aprite; with ci the subject named or left out, sì or no in front or not (Sì, Giulia ci va. Ci va.); la torta che fa
  la nonna and che la nonna fa; il libro che (io) leggo; Vorrei un gelato with or without per favore.
- **Extended** (`grading/it.json`, `GradingLanguagesTest`): ci and ne not elided before e are almost, with the
  apostrophe hint, as an article not elided already is: ce ne è for ce n'è (ci-ne asks for it typed), ci è for c'è,
  ci era for c'era (the imperfetto's). Ne before another word (ne ho tre) and ce ne sono keep their verdicts, and ci ne
  è stays wrong.
- **Accents and apostrophes stay almost** as in A1 (piu, si for sì; un aranciata).
- **A choice's `almost`** is a right form in the wrong register only: voglio for vorrei, ti and potresti to the teacher,
  Ti piacerebbe for Le piacerebbe. Every such choice keeps a wrong option besides (the validator's rule).
- **No distractor fits its gap in grammar**: no sempre where non … mai is asked (non abbaia sempre is Italian), no
  voglio where Io vorrei is, no dammi where dagli is (Sheets.check makes a distractor wrong even when it is a slip away).

## The read-aloud and the prints

- **Read aloud**: the bold of each explanation is Italian only; German in a step's explanation is in italics (*nie*,
  *der Hund, der im Garten bellt*), so Elena's voice never reads German (the smoke's course-speech check for Friuli).
  The intro's table cells in Italian are plain forms (ci vado, ne ho tre, Le do il quaderno), which the vocabulary of
  the read-aloud counts and the blackboard writes ("ci vado", "a lui gli", "il cane che abbaia").
- **The prints** (`companion/bin/school-prints --language it --level A2 --base de`): 6 sheets and the booklet (11
  pages: cover, contents, the units); pronomi-indiretti, vorrei and imperativo-pronomi take two pages, ci-ne, negazioni
  and relative-che one. Each sheet's "Achtung" pairs are its own: do gli / gli do, la telefono / Le telefono, gli non
  dico / non gli dico (pronomi-indiretti); ho sette / ne ho sette, ci n'è / ce n'è, ne vengo / ci vengo (ci-ne); dami /
  dammi, non toccalo / non toccarlo, daggli / dagli (imperativo-pronomi); il libro leggo / il libro che leggo, chi / che,
  gioca / giocano (relative-che); a choice's wrong option for the others. The words without a meaning in the packs (9 to
  18 a unit, as A1's 5 to 13: verbs like guardare, adverbs like mai) are left off the sheet, which still lists 12 to 22.
  Previews: scratchpad/ux-course-it-a2c/prints-de/previews/.

## For the native check

Every unit and page is marked machine-written (`review`). What most needs an Italian speaker's eye (and a German one's
for the child's German, a Slovene one's for the Slovene):

- **pronomi-indiretti**: Le with a capital in Maestra, Le do il quaderno; gli for a loro (Gli racconto una storia, and
  Racconto loro una storia accepted); la storia del lupo; alla pecora Bianca → le; Le telefono ogni sera.
- **ci-ne**: Avete del montasio? Mi dispiace, oggi non ne ho; Ne ha sei for Lorenzo's age; Giulia va al mare e ci va
  con la nonna; ce n'è un pezzo; ci n'è as the mistake shown.
- **negazioni**: Zia Nives sente poco; non spegne mai la sua radio; Nessuno ride; un po' sorted with niente as its
  opposite; Il pranzo non è ancora pronto.
- **vorrei**: voglio as almost; Che cosa vorreste? from Marisa to two children; Che cosa vorresti, bella? from the ice
  cream man; Signora Marisa, Lei vorrebbe un caffè?; Le piacerebbe venire alla nostra festa?
- **imperativo-pronomi**: Prendimi! in a game of tag; Rompile nella ciotola; dalla, falla in the table; Mi scusi, Mi
  dica on the page.
- **relative-che**: la torta che fa la nonna and che la nonna fa; Vedo una barca che è rossa (a transform that asks for
  che where Italian would say una barca rossa); the riddles.
- The Slovene of the examples is mostly masculine (Teo's), as A1's.
