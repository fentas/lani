**What people say: please say which are really said, and where.** The rule protects what people say in British English as the Lake District hears it, and a form from elsewhere is a learner's mistake unless a source ties it here (followups-12.md). Only `stood` and `sat` have a source; the rest is my own knowledge, so **unsourced**.

- **New in `nonstandard`, almost with the standard form shown:**
  - stood for standing, sat for sitting, after am, is, are or a noun (The man stood by the gate is Jack, I'm sat here). Sourced: Northern and South-Western English, spreading ([Stange 2014, English Language and Linguistics](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/english-language-and-linguistics/article/abs/i-was-sat-there-talking-all-night-a-corpusbased-study-on-factors-governing-intradialectal-variation-in-british-english1/119B4057E172AB1C648FC4EF0673B905), [a study of pseudo-passives with sit and stand](https://methodsxvii.uni-mainz.de/files/2022/06/Stange-Id-just-be-stood-there-doing-nothing.-A-corpus-based-study-on-recent-changes-in-the-use-of-pseudo-passives-with-SIT-and-STAND.pdf)), read as search extracts, not opened. Part a has the rows after was, were and been, which are different rows: they can stand side by side.
  - who for whom after a small word (Mr Tyson, to who I spoke; both of who). `to who` is what the transform takes away, so it is almost, not right.
  - what for which with two words before it (It rained all day, what meant we stayed in). B1 has what after a noun.
  - neither for either and none for any after a negative (I don't like neither of them, I haven't got none).
  - neither … or for nor (Neither Jack or Sam is coming).
  - the both of them for both of them.
- **Right, labelled (an item's `spoken`):** My bike has got stolen; to not touch, to not go, warned us to not walk (B1 has told me to not); Each of them have a name; Neither of the dogs are here, Neither Jack nor Sam are coming; I don't like neither tea nor coffee, on the one line that asks it.

## How the units are written

As COURSE.md "Writing a unit" and "The English course", plus what these six found (for COURSE.md's section, once part c has integrated):

- **The grading strips commas**, so a comma can only be tested by a choice, and only a prompt with no gap is safe, since a gap's choice may be typed once the rule is secure (`Forms.gap`, Adaptive). `EnglishB2bContentTest` holds that for `non-defining`. A typed line tests the words (who, which, whose), never the commas.
- **A subject that can be read either way lets that in.** The mill that was built in 1800 is plainly right with no commas, so a line that asks which against that needs a name (the Packhorse, Hill Top), or every right answer has to be listed.
- **A question about a sentence** goes in the `instruction`, the sentence in the prompt. One prompt in two languages is read whole in her English voice, because `spokenLooks` counts more English words than the base's. The smoke caught this for Italian only, because of its word list; a script of this part found the same trouble in the other two.
- **Italics in a base's text go to her voice if the word looks English**: German *was*, Italian *a*. Plain, they stay in the base.
- **The Watch out box reads only English `never`**, in the English text. Each base writes its own (nikoli, nie, mai).
- **A wrong option can be right but silly**, and the box prints it as a mistake (Take both of them: they're both good). The prompt has to rule it out: it doesn't matter which.
- **A blacksmith doesn't mend boots**, and Mr Tyson's mill is running in the cast, so it can't be closed or for sale. Read a line against the villager's file.
- **A word in a unit about it can't do other jobs**: "both want the singular" in `both-neither` reads as a rule.
- **The rule line keeps a sentence only with a bold form and a colon** (`condense`, score 1.5), and a sentence ends at a bracket or a plain full stop, not at `.**`. So "**A.** (One son.) With none: **B.** (More than one.)" prints both halves, and "**A.** (One son.) **B.** (More than one.)" prints one. Read every line with `ruleLine`, because the smoke only asks that one bold form survives.
- **A long last step can leave its question out of QA's reach:** six rows of three-line cells and four examples put the field past `text_field(3)`. The table's cells can just name the form, as the examples say the whole sentence.
- **QA needs the learner's data for a base:** without `LANI_DEV_DATA` from make-learner.ts `--base de`, the dev bridge serves every text per base in Slovene, though the `say` shows German.
