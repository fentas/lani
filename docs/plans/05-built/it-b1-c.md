# Built: the Italian school's B1 units 13–18 (2026-11-01)

Teo (a child of about seven, learning Italian from German, in the Friuli village; a tablet) has A1 and A2 written, and
his aim becomes B1 at his next age. B1 was outlined in `companion/course/it/course.json` on 2026-11-01: 18 units, every
one planned, so once A2 was done Maestra Elena only said she was preparing the next lessons. Three branches write B1
side by side, in the outline's order: `it-b1-a` units 1–6 (trapassato to periodo-ipotetico), `it-b1-b` units 7–12
(pronomi-combinati to indefiniti) and this branch, `it-b1-c`, units 13–18, with the outline's titles. The format and
how a unit is written are companion/COURSE.md ("Writing a unit", "The Italian course" and its sections; this part's is
"The Italian course: B1, units 13–18").

## The units

Each has a guided intro of five or six steps (Maestra Elena's lines in German first with du, then Slovene and English;
every Italian form in bold for her voice with its meaning in brackets after it, German in italics; two examples a step,
the table growing row by row, a quick question each), three worksheets from easy to harder (a table or a sort and
choices, then a gap text with a word bank, then transform lists) and a test at pass 80 with new sentences. B1's
grammar, but for a child: the stories and the village's life, the cast and tu, never the child's own data, no trick.

A2's units have 26–30 worksheet items and tests of 11–13: these are the same size (verbi-pronominali's first sheet a
table of the two verbs' six persons).

- **verbi-pronominali**: andarsene's two little words with the person (me ne vado, te ne vai, se ne va; mi, ti, si
  become me, te, se before ne), the plural (ce ne andiamo, ve ne andate, se ne vanno), non first; farcela (ce la
  faccio, ce la fai, ce la fa: only fare changes) and Non ce la faccio più!; ci vuole for one thing or a time, ci vogliono
  for more; the past: se n'è andato, se n'è andata with essere, and ce l'ho fatta, fatta for every speaker (a boy too).
  The gap text: a race to the pond; the test's: Giulia and Nonna Rosa's gubana.
- **discorso-indiretto**: dice che with the person changed (Sono stanco → Lorenzo dice che è stanco), mio → suo and
  ti → mi when it was said to you; chiede se for a yes-or-no question, the question word kept (chiede dove vado);
  dice di + the infinitive for an order (mi dice di lavarmi le mani); ha detto che with the imperfetto for what was so
  then (ieri … aveva freddo), the present for what is still to come (Ha detto che viene domani, the title, as Italians
  say it; verrà accepted). The gap text: Zia Nives can't hear over her radio, and {learner} tells her what everyone
  says; the test's: Giulia tells Nonna Rosa her day.
- **passivo**: essere + the participle agreeing like an adjective (il frico è fatto, la gubana è fatta, i biscotti sono
  fatti, le pecore sono contate), the doer after da and its melted forms (dal mugnaio, dalla maestra, dalle api), the
  past with both participles agreeing (la torta è stata mangiata, il vaso è stato rotto), an ordinary sentence turned
  round (Moro ha mangiato la torta → la torta è stata mangiata da Moro), venire + the participle for the present (è
  fatto and viene fatto both accepted). The gap text: the mystery of Nonna Rosa's gubana, eaten by Moro under the
  linden; the test's: the village ready for the festa.
- **infinito-preposizioni**: a after starting and learning (comincia a leggere, impara a nuotare, Davide insegna ai
  bambini a fischiare), after going, coming, helping, trying, going on; di after finishing, stopping, trying hard,
  deciding, forgetting (finisce di mangiare, smetti di correre) and after avere paura, avere voglia, essere contento;
  nothing after the modals, sapere, piacere, preferire; per (in order to) and prima di. No gap has an infinitive that
  starts with a vowel, so a and ad never compete (ad is on the page).
- **futuro-anteriore**: what will be done first, avrò … avranno + the participle (Quando avrò finito i compiti,
  gioco), sarò + the participle agreeing for going and coming (the speaker named: Lorenzo sarò arrivato, Giulia sarò
  arrivata), after quando, appena, dopo che, the other half in the future or, as people say it, the present (both
  accepted, either order); a guess about the past (Sarà stato Moro! Dove sarà andato?). The test: Nonno Bepi's
  birthday.
- **congiunzioni**: perché after the main sentence and siccome first (Siccome piove, restiamo a casa), quindi, allora,
  così, ma and però, invece, anche se with the indicative (Anche se piove, Davide va al pascolo), mentre, appena;
  benché, sebbene and prima che named on the page as the subjunctive's, not practised. Every gap where more than one
  word fits lists each one (Siccome, Poiché, Dato che, Visto che); ma and però, which nearly always both fit, are in
  choices only.

They assume A1, A2 and B1's units 1–12 in the outline's order, which the other two parts write; around a gap they use
B1's earlier units only lightly (the pages of those units aren't on this branch, so no unit here names them): the
reflexives (lavarmi), the modals, ci and ne (verbi-pronominali builds on ci-ne), the participles of their own (rotto,
scritto, messo in the passive), the imperfetto (ha detto che aveva fame), the future (futuro-anteriore builds on it).

## The grammar pages

Six new B1 pages in the Italian book (`companion/grammar/it/`), each the book's usual size (a rule per base of about
1,000 to 1,200 characters, a table of eight to ten rows, eight examples, notes on the ones that teach), in it, sl, en
and de: `verbi-pronominali`, `discorso-indiretto`, `passivo`, `infinito-preposizioni`, `futuro-anteriore`,
`congiunzioni`. The outline names each unit's pages (`grammar`): its own first (the summary card), then the earlier ones
it builds on. The pages go a little further than the units where the book should: metterci and non te la prendere,
the written che verrà, ad before a vowel, benché, sebbene and prima che with the subjunctive. A page's English says its
mistakes as "(never *…*)", which the print's "Achtung" takes. The book has 40 Italian pages now.

## Grading

- **Every right answer is graded right** by the Italian grading, and every other right answer is listed: è fatto and
  viene fatto; a reported question or order with and without its speaker and mi or ci (Tommaso mi chiede se ho una
  palla. Chiede se ho una palla.); fa and farà after la radio dice che domani; joined sentences in either order; the
  main clause in the future or the present after quando avrò finito; the conjunctions that fit a gap.
- **Extended** (`grading/it.json`, `GradingLanguagesTest`): an articulated preposition for another is wrong, never a
  slip. "Il miele è fatto delle api" for "dalle api" was almost (one letter off, in the stem) for the very mistake the
  passive teaches; so were alla and nella for dalla. The known mistakes (`wrong`) now list the five-letter articulated
  forms of di, da, in, a and su (della, dalla, nella, alla, sulla and their -o, -e, -gli forms): typed for another,
  wrong. The three-letter ones (del, dal) were wrong already (no typo under four letters). A slip in the word itself
  (dlla) or in the word beside it stays forgiven; a real word typed for one of them is wrong too now (della for bella,
  alla for palla: rare, and a different word). The README, module-spec, the fix skill and the tutor's guide topic "fix"
  (`tutor-export`) say it.
- **Needing no change**: mi ne vado for me ne vado, ce l'ho fatto for ce l'ho fatta, ci vuole for ci vogliono, a for
  di, comincia di, finisce a, the unchanged person (Lorenzo dice che sono stanco), averò, a participle of essere that
  doesn't agree (sarò arrivato for Giulia, è stato mangiata), Quando ho finito for avrò finito are wrong; ce la ho
  fatta and se ne è andata are almost (the apostrophe, as ce ne è already is); a missing accent stays almost.
- **A choice's wrong option** is wrong Italian (mi ne, ci ne, ha stato) or wrong by its own sentence (la mia where
  Davide is told about, sono where Giulia said it, se ne va for two children).
- **No distractor fits its gap**: rompito and stato in the passive's bank, fatto and vogliamo in verbi-pronominali's,
  ho, il mio and a in Zia Nives's (no che: chiede che veniamo is Italian too), da and con in infinito-preposizioni's,
  avrà and averò in futuro-anteriore's; congiunzioni's has none, as vorrei's has none. delle in the passive's bank is
  almost by Grading alone for dalle; the sheet makes a distractor wrong, and the grading change makes it wrong typed.

## How it was written

This branch wrote units 13–15 itself and gave 16–18 to a helper (a fork of the same session, files only, no commits),
both to the same rules; every unit was then checked by the same validators and grading port. A second reader (a fresh
agent, read only, with the files and COURSE.md but not the writers' reasoning) read every item. It found 4 must-fix points, 12 should-fix and some nits; all were taken but one, the
hints that give a conjunction's or a preposition's meaning (weil, ob, bevor), kept as A2's are (ci's hint is
"dorthin"):

- **Right answers marked wrong, or a second answer that fit**: «La lettera è scritta ___ maestra.» took alla too
  (written to the teacher: the prompt now asks «Chi scrive la lettera?» first); «Sono state mangiate ___ formiche?»
  took the bank's da and the distractor delle (some ants, the partitive: da accepted, dai the distractor); Dopo che
  after appena; the bank's avrà fit futuro-anteriore's first gap (no subject showed it was Giulia: sarò now); the joined
  sentences with the subject moved to the main sentence; se ne è andata, standard unelided, beside se n'è andata.
- **perché in front**: less usual, not wrong (Treccani: siccome or poiché preferred there), so the intro's choice
  takes it as almost, the test's option is invece instead, and the page says siccome is the usual word, not "never".
- **A sort that gave its answers away** (the passive test's labels were its words' first words): by agreement now.
- **Two subjects** in "prima di mangiare, deve cuocere": "deve cuocere un'ora prima di essere pronta".
- **The cast and a child's day**: Lorenzo gets the flour at his grandfather's mill, not buys it; at the sea Nonno Bepi
  reads the paper and the water is only cold.
- **Words**: "pass on" for "tell on" (to snitch); der Gegensatz for das Gegenteil; the German page of infinito names
  the verbs for "nach Anfangen, Lernen"; forms in bold for Elena's voice (essere, avere, imperfetto, dal nonno); the
  Slovene Ljubi se mi igrati, {m:Lačen|f:Lačna} sem.
