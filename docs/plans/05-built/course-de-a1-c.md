# Built: the German school's last six A1 units, 13–18 (2026-11-01)

Jan, 2026-11-01: "can you make app complete - all what is missing?" There was no German course: a learner of German in
the Carinthian village found the school empty. Three branches write A1 side by side, in the outline's order:
`course-de-a1-a` units 1–6 and the shared groundwork, `course-de-a1-b` units 7–12, and this branch (`course-de-a1-c`)
units 13–18: possessiv, uhrzeit, moechten, trennbare, imperativ and perfekt. The format and how a unit is written are in
companion/COURSE.md: "Writing a unit", "The German course", and its "units 1–6" and "units 13–18" sections.

The course is for adults, future learners and visitors, from every base German has here: Slovene, English and
Italian. All of it is machine-written and flagged for a native check (below).

## What, and how to use it

The school of a Carinthian village (`LANI_CULTURE=kaernten`, a learner of German from sl, en or it) opens these six
units after unit 12, kein-nicht. Each unit is played as the Italian A1 units are: Lehrerin Eva's five-step intro, read
aloud, then three worksheets, then the test. Each unit prints as an A4 sheet: 📄 in the unit, or `GET
/course/sheet/<unit>`. No screen changed, so tap counts are the same as before: the school's unit opens with 1 tap
from the school, and the intro, the sheets and the test with 1 tap each.

What each unit teaches:

- **possessiv**: mein and dein end like ein (mein Hund, meine Katze, meine Schafe). The owner picks sein or ihr, and
  the noun picks the ending. Then unser, euer and eure, ihr (their) and Ihr (polite), -en in the accusative (seinen
  Hammer) and a name's -s (Kathis Gasthaus, Franz' Mühle). The gap text is set in Kathi's inn; the test is about
  Stanko and his bees.
- **uhrzeit**:
  - the hour: Wie spät ist es?, Es ist sieben Uhr, ein Uhr or eins;
  - halb acht is 7:30. It counts towards the next hour, as the Slovene pol osmih does, while the British half eight
    is 8:30;
  - Viertel nach and Viertel vor, with the Austrian viertel acht and dreiviertel acht, and the minutes;
  - when: um, am, im, the days, in der Früh, Jänner, heuer, and the verb second after a time.

  The gap text is Kathi's week (Ruhetag on Monday); the test is Florian's day on the pasture.
- **moechten**:
  - Ich möchte to ask for something (Was möchten Sie?);
  - möchten and können in every person (ich = er, no -t), Stanko kann Slowenisch;
  - the other verb at the end;
  - questions, nicht, Kann ich zahlen?, and the Austrian Ich hätte gern.

  The gap text is set at the inn; the test is Opa Sepp's story of the Lindwurm.
- **trennbare**: the prefix goes to the end (Ich stehe um sieben auf): einkaufen, anrufen, mitkommen. Then questions
  and nicht, the Austrian aufsperren and zusperren, the verb kept whole after möchten and können, and be-, ver-, er-,
  which never come off. The gap text is Stefan's day; the test is Lehrerin Eva's morning.
- **imperativ**:
  - komm!, kommt!, kommen Sie!; the -e where Duden has it (komme!), and warte!;
  - gib!, nimm!, lies! keep their vowel change, but fahr! and schlaf! drop theirs;
  - sei!, seid!, seien Sie!; bitte, mal, doch;
  - steh auf!, komm mit!, and nicht.

  The gap text is set in Oma Resi's kitchen; the test is set in Kathi's inn.
- **perfekt**:
  - haben + ge-…-t; ge-…-en, learned with the verb;
  - sein for going and coming, and the Austrian ist gesessen;
  - the participle at the end;
  - ge in the middle of a separable verb, and none with be-, ver- or -ieren.

  The gap text is a Kirchtag Sunday; the test is a market day in Villach.

Around their gaps, the units use only the grammar of the units before them in the outline. Places are the fixed chunks
of units 1–6 (im Gasthaus, auf der Alm, in der Früh). There is no dative.

## The grammar pages

German had 16 pages, none of them for whose, the time, or the imperative. Three new A1 pages are written in the format
of part a's zahlen: the rule, the table, examples with notes, and a hand-written cheat-sheet card with a tip.

- **`possessiv`**: the eight possessives with their endings; the owner picks the word, the noun picks the ending;
  -en in the accusative; euer loses an e; a name's -s. It has two notes: for Slovene (German has no svoj: Toni sucht
  seinen Hammer) and for Italian (the owner decides, not the thing; no article).
- **`uhrzeit`**: the hour; halb; Viertel nach and vor beside the Austrian viertel acht and dreiviertel acht; the
  minutes; the timetable's sieben Uhr fünfzehn; um, am, im; the days; in der Früh, Jänner and heuer, with the
  German-German forms named.
- **`imperativ`**: du, ihr and Sie; the -e; e→i kept, a→ä dropped; sein; bitte, mal and doch; separable verbs. The
  page was written by this branch's helper.

`modalverben`, `trennbare-verben` and `perfekt` are the book's existing A2 pages, named as they are, as the Italian A1
units name A2 pages.

## Grading (grading/de.json)

Every right answer of the six units is graded right. Each new known mistake applies only to a word of its group, so a
keyboard slip on any other word stays almost. The new pairs:

- mein, dein, sein, ein and kein typed for each other, with any ending (dein for mein, seinen for meinen). They are
  one letter apart, so the app forgave them before as a slip in the stem.
- mochte for möchte, which is the past of mögen. moechte stays almost, with the hint umlaut.
- The present's umlaut on an imperative or on ich's form that has none: fähr! for fahr!, läuf! for lauf!, ich fähre.
- A strong participle with the infinitive's vowel: getrinken, gesprechen, geschreiben, gebleiben, mitgenehmen.
- ge- where none goes (getelefoniert, gebesucht, geverstanden), and a separable participle without its ge (anrufen
  for angerufen, einkauft for eingekauft, aufstanden for aufgestanden). A slip that keeps the ge (telefonirt) stays
  almost.

GradingLanguagesTest's German section has three new tests for them. Ihr and ihr are one answer, as Sie and sie are:
the polite Ihr is asked in a choice. The answers list every right form: the imperative's optional -e, unseren beside
unsren and unsern, the Austrian times beside the German ones, and the timetable's numbers.

## Native check

Austrian German first:

- **uhrzeit**:
  - Is it true that Carinthia says viertel acht and dreiviertel acht (also written drei viertel acht)? Both are
    accepted beside Viertel nach sieben and Viertel vor acht.
  - in der Früh as the village's word; heuer.
  - "Das Gasthaus hat am Montag Ruhetag".
  - halb drei for 14:30 in speech.
  - "Kasnudeln … um zwölf sind sie fertig".
- **possessiv**:
  - Franz' Mühle; euer and eure; unseren beside unsren and unsern.
  - Kathis Gasthaus without an apostrophe (Stanko's is not accepted).
  - Oma Resi bringt ihren Reindling.
- **moechten**:
  - zwei Glas Most (the measure in the singular).
  - Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?
  - Ich hätte gern as the Austrian counter phrase.
  - Stanko kann Slowenisch; the Lindwurm story.
- **trennbare**:
  - aufsperren and zusperren as the Austrian standard (Germany: aufschließen, abschließen).
  - "Florian kommt mit nach Villach", with fährt accepted too.
  - "macht den Keller zu", accepted beside "sperrt zu".
- **imperativ**:
  - the -e accepted on Komme!, Mache!, Hole!, Bringe!, Fahre!, Schlafe!;
  - Schau mal! as Austrian (Germany: guck);
  - "Sperr bitte auf!", "Komm nicht zu spät!", "Stefan, fahr nicht zu schnell!".
- **perfekt**:
  - ist gesessen as the Austrian standard (hat gesessen in Germany). It appears only in a choice and in the intro's
    rows, never as a typed answer.
  - The Perfekt of sein where Germany says war ("Am Sonntag ist im Dorf Kirchtag gewesen", "Gestern ist Markttag
    gewesen").
  - "Opa Sepp ist bis Mitternacht im Gasthaus geblieben".
- **The three pages**: the rule lines and the cards' tips in each base.

Then each base:

- **Slovene**:
  - möchte as bi or želim, avoiding rad bi or rada bi. Where a translation needs a gender, it gives both (plaval,
    plavala; prišel, prišla). "Danes bi šel plavat" is masculine only.
  - the svoj notes (Toni išče svoje kladivo).
  - the duals in translations (vajin pes, vajina žoga; počakajta, jejta).
  - četrt na osem and tri četrt na osem as the match for the Austrian times.
  - the verb aspect in the Perfekt translations (pojedli, pili, prodajala).
- **Italian**:
  - "suo" by the owner (la sua gatta for Kathi's Katze);
  - le sette e mezza for halb acht;
  - "Da'!" for Gib!; "cotto al forno" for gebacken; "la stube" kept as a word.
- **English**: British usage (half past seven, the alpine pasture, cider for Most, the inn for the Gasthaus).

Every explanation, why and translation in en, sl and it is machine-written. Each file's `review` field names its own
check items.
